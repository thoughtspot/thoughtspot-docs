Instructions are the system prompt that defines an agent's behavior. They are the single highest-leverage thing a builder writes. This article shows the structure that consistently produces reliable agents.

== Why instructions matter

The model behind your agent is general-purpose. Instructions are how you turn it into a specific assistant with a specific job, scope, voice, and fallback behavior. Same data, same model, different instructions → different agents.

Two rules:

. *Be specific.* Vague instructions produce vague behavior.
. *Be short.* Long prompts get partially ignored. Aim for under 300 words.

== The five-section structure

Every agent instruction should have these five sections, in this order:

. *Role* — one sentence on who the agent is and who it serves.
. *What to do* — the kinds of questions or tasks it handles.
. *What not to do* — out-of-scope topics, sensitive areas, escalation rules.
. *Tone and format* — how answers should sound and look.
. *When unsure* — what to do when the answer is not in the data.

== Each section in detail

=== Role

One sentence. Names the agent, who uses it, and what kind of help it provides.

* Weak: `You are a helpful assistant.`
* Strong: `You are the EMEA Pipeline Assistant. You help EMEA sales managers and RevOps understand the state of the EMEA sales pipeline.`

=== What to do

A short list of what is in scope. Specific topics, specific data, specific actions.

* Weak: `Answer questions about sales.`
* Strong:
+
[source,text]
----
You can:
- Answer questions about open opportunities, stages, forecasts, and
  historical close rates for EMEA accounts only.
- Compare regions, segments, and time periods within EMEA.
----

=== What not to do

Equally short. Naming what's out of scope is what stops the agent from making things up or answering things it shouldn't.

* Strong:
+
[source,text]
----
You cannot:
- Discuss compensation, individual rep performance reviews, or any
  non-EMEA region. If asked, say so and suggest contacting RevOps.
----

=== Tone and format

Three to five concrete rules. Don't say "be professional" — say what professional looks like.

* Weak: `Be clear and concise.`
* Strong:
+
[source,text]
----
Format:
- Be concise. Lead with the number.
- Use bullets for lists of accounts or stages.
- Always name the data source you used.
----

=== When unsure

The fallback rule. Without this, the agent will guess.

* Strong:
+
[source,text]
----
If the data does not support a confident answer, say "I don't have
enough data to answer that confidently" and stop. Do not guess.
----

== Worked example

[source,text]
----
You are the EMEA Pipeline Assistant. You help EMEA sales managers
and RevOps understand the state of the EMEA sales pipeline.

You can:
- Answer questions about open opportunities, stages, forecasts,
  and historical close rates for EMEA accounts only.
- Compare regions, segments, and time periods within EMEA.

You cannot:
- Discuss compensation, individual rep performance reviews, or any
  non-EMEA region. If asked, say so and suggest contacting RevOps.

Format:
- Be concise. Lead with the number.
- Use bullets for lists of accounts or stages.
- Always name the data source you used.

If the data does not support a confident answer, say "I don't have
enough data to answer that confidently" and stop. Do not guess.
----

Word count: ~120. That's enough.

== Prompt tips

* *Put the most important rules first.* If an agent ignores a rule, it's usually because the rule appeared late in a long prompt. Move it up.
* *Use lists, not paragraphs.* Models follow bulleted rules more reliably than prose.
* *Refer to data by name.* If you've attached a worksheet called `emea_pipeline_open`, mention it by name in the instructions ("answer pipeline questions using `emea_pipeline_open`").
* *Tell the agent what to do when it can't help.* "If asked about X, say Y and suggest Z." This prevents hallucinated answers and gives the user an offramp.
* *Don't restate the obvious.* The model knows it's a helpful assistant. You don't need to say so.

== Common anti-patterns

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Anti-pattern |Why it backfires

|"Always be accurate."
|Vacuous. Tell it *how* — e.g. "only answer from attached data; if not present, say you don't know."

|Long preamble before the first instruction.
|The first 50 words carry the most weight. Don't waste them on flavor text.

|Mixing instructions with data.
|Data belongs in the *Data* tab, not the prompt. The prompt should reference data, not contain it.

|Negative-only prompts.
|"Don't do X, don't do Y, don't do Z." Pair every "don't" with a "do."

|Telling the agent its model name or capabilities.
|Irrelevant to behavior. Removes attention from the rules that matter.
|===

== Iterating on instructions

. Test with 3–5 real questions plus one adversarial question.
. When the agent fails, ask why — wrong scope? wrong tone? guessed?
. Add or sharpen *one* instruction that addresses that failure.
. Re-test. Don't accumulate rules; the prompt should stay short.
