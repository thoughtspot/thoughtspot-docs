== Decision tree

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|If your problem is... |Contact...

|A specific agent gives a wrong or unexpected answer
|The agent's *Owner*, listed on the agent's detail page.

|You can't sign in, can't see any agents, can't see the *Admin* section you think you should
|Your AgentSpot admin.

|A connector is broken (auth failure, missing tools, weird errors)
|Your AgentSpot admin.

|A quota or guardrail is blocking valid use
|Your AgentSpot admin.

|You think you're seeing data you shouldn't
|Your AgentSpot admin — *immediately*. Don't share what you saw.

|Provisioning failed, tenant doesn't appear in *Features*, audit data missing, platform outage
|ThoughtSpot Support (your admin opens the case).

|You suspect a security incident
|Your AgentSpot admin AND your security team — same hour.

|General how-to questions
|The relevant section in these docs first. If still unclear, the agent's *Owner* or your admin.
|===

== What to bring when you report

Good bug reports get fixed fast. Bad ones get a back-and-forth that takes a week. The difference is the information you bring up front.

=== Reporting a wrong agent answer

To the agent's owner, in one message:

* *Agent name* (and version if visible).
* *The exact question* you asked.
* *The exact answer* the agent gave (copy-paste, don't paraphrase).
* *What you expected instead* and why.
* *Date and time* you asked.
* (If applicable) the *conversation share link*.

=== Reporting an access issue

To your admin, in one message:

* *Your email* (the one you sign in with).
* *What you were trying to do.*
* *What you saw* (error message verbatim, or "I see X but expected Y").
* *Browser and incognito-or-not.*
* *Date and time.*

=== Reporting a connector issue

To your admin:

* *Connector name.*
* *Agent or workflow* that triggered the issue.
* *Step name* (for workflows).
* *Error message verbatim.*
* *Date and time of the failed call.*
* *What worked before* (was this connector working yesterday?).

=== Reporting a possible security or privacy issue

To your admin (and your security team, if you have one):

* Treat as urgent — same business day, not a ticket queued for next week.
* *Don't* paste the data you saw into chat or email. Describe it instead ("I'm an Agent Consumer but I saw a record with PII that should be admin-only").
* *Do* share: the agent name, the question that triggered it, your role, the date/time, and (if possible) the conversation share link.
* Stop using the affected agent until the issue is confirmed resolved.

== Self-service before you escalate

Most issues have an answer in the docs. Spend 60 seconds checking:

* xref:01-common-issues-faq.adoc[Common issues FAQ] — covers the top 80% of issues.
* The agent's detail page — *What it can do*, *What it can't do*, *Owner*, *Last updated*.
* The relevant per-section overview in these docs (e.g. xref:../01-agents/01-overview.adoc[Agents overview], xref:../02-workflows/01-overview.adoc[Workflows overview]).
* The audit log entry for your specific failure — admins can find this in seconds and it often shows exactly why something blocked.

== Communication norms

A few small habits make the support load lighter for everyone:

* *One issue per message* — don't bundle three problems into one report. They get triaged separately.
* *Send the reproduction steps* — agents are non-deterministic; the same question can give different answers on different runs. Reproducible issues get fixed; "it sometimes does this" gets stuck.
* *Don't reopen a closed report* without new information. Open a new one and reference the old.
* *Acknowledge when something's fixed* — it helps the owner know whether to keep digging.

== Escalation timeline

If you've reported an issue and haven't heard back:

* *Agent or workflow issue:* 2 business days is reasonable; nudge the owner after that.
* *Access or connector issue:* 1 business day is reasonable; nudge your admin after that.
* *Security or privacy issue:* if you haven't heard back within the same business day, escalate to your admin's manager.

== Feedback that isn't a bug

Not everything is a bug. Sometimes you want to suggest a feature, a new agent, a new data source, a better workflow.

* *Inside a specific agent:* thumbs-up with a one-line note is the lowest-friction signal. The owner sees the pattern over time.
* *Across the platform:* talk to your AgentSpot admin. They can route to ThoughtSpot Product.

Don't wait for "the perfect feedback time." Small, frequent notes outperform a quarterly long document by a wide margin.
