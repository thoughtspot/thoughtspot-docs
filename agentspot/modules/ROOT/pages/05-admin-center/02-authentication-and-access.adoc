= Authentication and access
:last_updated: June 10, 2026
:linkattrs:

[.lead]
Configure single sign-on, map identity-provider groups to AgentSpot roles, and manage who can do what in your tenant.

== Overview

AgentSpot inherits your ThoughtSpot tenant's identity model. Users sign in with their corporate accounts via your IdP. Group membership in the IdP determines what role they hold in AgentSpot.

Three things to set up, in order:

. *SSO* — connect AgentSpot to your IdP.
. *Role mapping* — link IdP groups to AgentSpot roles.
. *Groups* — for finer-grained access to agents, workflows, and connectors.

== Configure SSO

If your ThoughtSpot tenant already uses SSO, AgentSpot inherits that configuration and you can skip to xref:#role-mapping[role mapping]. ⚠️ Confirm inheritance behaviour with [feature owner].

=== Connect a new identity provider

. Open *Admin* > *Authentication*. ⚠️ Confirm path.
. Select *Add identity provider*.
. Choose the protocol: *SAML 2.0* or *OIDC*.
. Copy the *Service Provider* metadata (entity ID, ACS URL, audience) shown on the page.
. In your IdP, create a new application using those values. Configure attribute statements to include at least:
+
* `email` (used as the AgentSpot user identifier)
* `firstName`, `lastName`
* `groups` (used for role mapping)
+
⚠️ Confirm exact attribute names with [feature owner].
. In your IdP, download the IdP metadata XML (SAML) or note the issuer URL and client credentials (OIDC).
. Back in AgentSpot, paste the IdP metadata or enter the OIDC values and save.
. Select *Test connection*. Sign in as a test user in a separate browser and confirm the round-trip succeeds.

*Expected result:* The test user is signed in via your IdP.

=== Supported identity providers

⚠️ Confirm the supported IdP list with [feature owner]. Typical: Okta, Azure AD / Entra ID, Google Workspace, Ping Identity, OneLogin, plus any SAML 2.0 / OIDC-compliant IdP.

[[role-mapping]]
== Map IdP groups to AgentSpot roles

. In *Admin* > *Authentication* > *Role mapping* (⚠️ confirm), select *Add mapping*.
. Enter the IdP group name (e.g. `agentspot-admins`).
. Select the AgentSpot role to assign.
. Save. Repeat for each group.

Group membership is evaluated at sign-in. A user removed from an IdP group loses the corresponding AgentSpot role at their next sign-in.

== Roles

⚠️ Confirm exact role names and capabilities with [feature owner]. Typical:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Role |Capabilities

|*AgentSpot Admin*
|Full tenant administration: configuration, users, connectors, model providers, governance, audit.

|*Agent Builder*
|Create, edit, publish, version agents and workflows. Cannot change tenant configuration.

|*Agent Consumer*
|Use published agents and workflows. Cannot create or edit.

|*AgentSpot Auditor*
|Read-only access to audit and usage. Cannot change anything.
|===

Use the smallest role that does the job. Don't grant *Admin* to everyone who asks for it.

== Groups

Groups control *visibility*: which agents, workflows, connectors, and data sources each user can see.

=== Built-in groups

* *All users* — implicit; every authenticated user.
* *All admins* — every user with an admin role.

=== Custom groups

Create custom groups for finer-grained access — *Finance*, *Sales-EMEA*, *Eng-Platform*, etc.

. Open *Admin* > *Groups* > *Create group*. ⚠️ Confirm path.
. Name the group.
. Choose membership: explicit user list, IdP group mapping, or both.
. Save.

Agent builders can then publish to specific groups; admins can share specific connectors with specific groups. See xref:03-connector-admin.adoc[Connector administration].

== Common access patterns

* *Tight start:* one admin group (mapped to `agentspot-admins` IdP group), one builder group (`agentspot-builders`), one consumer group (all signed-in users). Add finer groups as use cases emerge.
* *Per-team groups:* mirror your IdP groups (`sales-emea`, `cs-na`, `eng-platform`) so per-team agents land in the right hands automatically.
* *Pilot groups:* a temporary group (`agentspot-pilot-2026q3`) used to scope new connectors and agents during a pilot, then dissolved when the pilot ends.

== Service accounts

⚠️ Confirm support for service accounts in your tenant with [feature owner].

Use a service account when:

* A workflow needs to run on a schedule outside any human's identity.
* A connector should authenticate as a non-human identity (e.g. for tenant-wide writes).

Treat service-account credentials with the same care as any privileged credential — rotate regularly, restrict scope, audit usage.

== Deactivating users

Users are typically deactivated by removal from the IdP group. When that happens:

* They lose AgentSpot access at next sign-in.
* Their existing agents, workflows, and conversations remain — they don't get auto-deleted. Ownership transfer is a separate admin action.

⚠️ Confirm ownership-transfer and content-retention behaviour on deactivation with [feature owner].

== Troubleshooting

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Symptom |What to try

|SSO test sign-in: "user not provisioned".
|The user's IdP group isn't mapped to any AgentSpot role. Add a mapping or add the user to a mapped group.

|User has the right IdP group but no AgentSpot access.
|Group membership is evaluated at sign-in. Ask them to sign out and back in.

|Role mapping change doesn't take effect.
|Same — re-sign-in required.

|Custom group doesn't appear when publishing an agent.
|The group has zero members, or the builder isn't a member themselves. ⚠️ Confirm visibility behaviour.

|Test sign-in succeeds but real users get redirected back to the IdP repeatedly.
|Attribute mapping mismatch — most often `email` not being passed as the user identifier. Re-check IdP attribute statements.
|===
