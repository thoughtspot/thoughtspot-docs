[NOTE]
====
For to_date functions, we support the following codes:

[options="header"]
|===
| Code | Meaning | Example

| %Y | 4-digit year | 2026

| %y | 2-digit year | 26

| %m | 2-digit month | 05

| %d | 2-digit day | 03

| %b | abbreviated month name | Oct

| %a | abbreviated weekday | Thu

| %H / %I | 2-digit hour, 24-hour / 12-hour | 14 / 02

| %M | 2-digit minute | 05

| %S | 2-digit second | 09
|===

Other strptime codes (for example %B, %j, %p) are not supported.

Any combination works. The codes can appear in any order, and each separator can differ, as long as the format follows the value. For example, to_date('12-02:2026', '%d-%m:%Y').

Pad numbers to their full width. Use 4-digit years with %Y, and 2-digit years, months, days and time parts. Values with no separators (20261029) require this, and some warehouses (for example, Databricks) reject single-digit parts such as 2026-5-3.

Two-digit years (%y): the warehouse decides the century, and the cutoff differs between warehouses. Prefer %Y.

Letters are not separators. For example, the T in 2026-10-29T14:05 is not supported.

Warehouse-native patterns are not supported. For example 'yyyymmdd' or 'YYYY-MM-DD'. They may appear to work on some connections but return wrong dates or errors on others: on Databricks and Denodo mm means minutes, and BigQuery accepts only % codes.
